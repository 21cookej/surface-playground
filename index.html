A world with no “off the edge”

The red arrow lives on a two-dimensional surface. Movement follows that surface, including the underside, steep walls, and smoothly joined shapes.

Surface view unfolds geodesics: each screen direction launches a path tangent to the surface. Screen distance is distance travelled along that path. Curved paths can return to the arrow, creating repeated images.

3D observer shows the actual surface from behind you. Drag it to orbit. Press V to exchange the views.

Orange is positive Gaussian curvature; blue is negative; gray is zero. A cylinder is gray despite bending in 3D: its intrinsic geometry is flat. A torus changes from orange outside to blue inside, crossing zero along two circles.

Try the cylinder to see repeated versions of yourself. The two-plane surface connects two infinite sheets through a smooth circular throat. Raise its depth to increase the distance between them.

Numerical surface geodesics, not a full 4D renderer. The view has a finite travel range; it is an unfolded ray map, not a camera floating over the world. Very distant paths use a sampled mesh and may show interpolation artifacts.
